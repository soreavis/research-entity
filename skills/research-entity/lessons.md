# Lessons from Production Runs

Patterns observed across real-world dossiers and the rules that prevent them. All identifying details have been generalized; the patterns are universal.

## Data validation lessons

1. **Tavily quota exhaustion (HTTP 432)** — fall back gracefully to WebSearch + WebFetch; never fail the whole run on Tavily failure.
2. **Aggregator data is unreliable** — Latka, RocketReach, Tracxn, PitchBook, Crunchbase routinely disagree on the same company's revenue / customer count / employee count. Always require ≥2 independent sources or label `single-source`.
3. **Aggregator-only employee count** — a real company's headcount varied across `85, 130, 133, 134, 135, 250–500, 51–200 band` depending on which aggregator. Honest answer: "no multi-source-verified figure; LinkedIn band X–Y is the most defensible upper-bound."
4. **Latka data is founder-interview self-reported** — never independently audited. Treat as single-source.
5. **Investor name not actually the named investor** — an aggregator listed an investor whose name closely matched a real, prominent VC, but the actual VC's disclosed portfolio did not include the company. The named "investor" may be a different firm, an alias, or never have existed as registered. Verify investor identity by checking the investor's own portfolio page.

## Founder narrative lessons

6. **Founder-exit narrative inflation** — a popular pattern: a founder's bio implies they "sold" or "built" a company that achieved a $XXX million exit. The reality often turns out to be that the founder's company was a contractor / technology developer for a separate legal entity (founded by someone else, backed by other investors), and the equity proceeds went to the equity holders, not the contractor. Always cross-check the exit narrative against Wikipedia + mainstream business press + the acquirer's announcement + the equity-holding investor's exit press release. Don't repeat misframed narratives.
7. **Bio dates vs. business register dates** — a founder's bio dated a subsidiary "founded 2001" but the official Commercial Register showed formal incorporation in 2004. The earlier date may reflect informal predecessor activity. Always check the legal register and document conflicts in §2 with both dates and the most likely reconciliation.
8. **Stale-address-in-bio pattern** — a real-world case: the founder's published bio listed an address that the current business-register entry no longer matched (the company had moved). Always cross-check the registered seat against the current public-register entry, not the founder's bio.

## Temporal claim lessons

9. **First-mover overclaim** — an entity's marketing framed it as "first mainstream X with Y" but verified competitors had shipped Y 9–11 months earlier. Always verify temporal claims by searching the top 5 competitors before publishing.
10. **AI feature / capability claims** require checking the relevant standards-body registry (e.g., the Anthropic MCP registry for MCP servers, the FedRAMP marketplace for FedRAMP claims, the SOC 2 disclosures register for SOC 2 claims) and the top 5 competitors before publishing as a first-mover claim.

## Voice / format lessons

11. **Editorial trail leakage** — first revision left "Key correction", "Important correction", "NEW in this revision" markers. Final document must be a single source of truth in declarative voice. Fix facts and move on; don't narrate the correction.
12. **Side-by-side mermaid subgraphs** — produced an unreadable landscape diagram on first pass. Switched to a vertical chain of subgraphs with `<b>` tags inside nodes (NOT `**...**`, which mermaid renders literally) and `<br/>` for line breaks.

## Corporate-structure lessons

13. **Lightweight subsidiary entity vs. operating reality** — a real-world case: the legal subsidiary (per the foreign business register) showed "5–9 employees" and small revenue, but operationally the parent was running a much larger team via the parent structure. Don't conflate the legal-entity headcount with the operational-team size; document both.
14. **Domain rebrand 301-redirect signal** — when a company rebrands, the legacy domain typically 301-redirects. Confirm the redirect chain works and that all legacy URLs (press releases, case studies) resolve cleanly. Update §15 Press timeline to note rebrand-era URL changes.
15. **Multiple legal entities under one trade name** — a company often operates as US Inc. + foreign GmbH + foreign s.r.o. + branding entity, with parent-subsidiary relationships that aggregators don't capture. The §3.4 Related Entities mermaid is mandatory — distinguishes founded / employed / contracted relationships.

## Visualization & UX lessons

16. **`quadrantChart` (and `sankey` / `mindmap` / `xychart-beta`) fail to render in many markdown viewers — hard ban, not soft preference.** A real production case: a `--depth=quick` AcmeCRM dossier shipped with §10 as a `quadrantChart`. The user's markdown viewer (and many basic renderers — default browser preview, GitHub-rendered MD, several IDE previewers, pandoc-md-to-pdf) showed the **mermaid source as raw code instead of rendering the chart**. Even when these blocks DO render, label-overlap in dense quadrants (5+ vendors) makes them unreadable. **Rule:** never ship a `quadrantChart` in a dossier. Use either (a) a 2×2 markdown table with axis labels in row-1 / col-1 headers and vendors listed inside cells (most portable — renders in every viewer), or (b) a `flowchart TB` with one subgraph per quadrant + TOP→MID→BOT vertical chain encoding the y-axis. Always pair with a "How to read this" caption explaining strategic implication. Static-validation Check 4b enforces this. (`mermaid-validation.md` rule #7 + Check 4b)

17. **Decision trees without scoring frameworks feel arbitrary.** A real production case: the §17 "should you evaluate?" decision tree was qualitative ("Strong fit" / "Mismatch") — readers asked "but what's the actual fit-score?" The fix is a 4-table scoring framework below the diagram: decision criteria gates + outcome scoring (5 weighted dimensions to 100 pts) + outcome bands (fit-score range + confidence color + recommendation) + how-to-apply (with the disclaimer that fit-score is directional, not predictive).

## Source-discipline lessons

18. **Founder origin-story claims (teenage businesses, conference-inspired-genesis) are structurally unverifiable.** A real production case: a founder bio claimed first business at 16 selling t-shirts and photography studio at 18. Multiple sources corroborated, but ALL sources were interview-based retellings of the founder's own narrative — no third-party documentation possible (a 1977 teenage business would never be in any register). Label as `founder self-claim` with explicit note that "corroboration is interview-based, not independently documented."

19. **First-mention citations matter as much as cumulative count.** A real production case: §1 Executive Summary referenced 6 hard facts (founder birth, executive tenure, ISO certifications, review-platform rating, customer count, content-platform metrics) but linked none of them inline; all were sourced elsewhere in the document. Add inline links to §1 first-mentions even if redundant with later sections — §1 is the natural reference point for casual readers who don't read the full document.

20. **Internal arithmetic consistency must be verified.** A real production case: the same cumulative AI-native cohort funding total appeared as 4 different numbers across the document ($72.5M / $267.5M / $272.5M+ / "~120×"). The post-fix audit revealed: $72.5M was a stale number from before Reevo + Lightfield + Monaco were added; correct number is $272.5M. Always grep for the same numeric across the document and reconcile drift.

21. **Glossary gaps surface only on audit.** A real production case: the §22 Glossary had 31 entries, but a body-scan revealed 24 additional acronyms / jargon terms used in the document without definitions (BLUF, SWOT, GTM, PE, VC, API, SDK, KPI, RFP, NDA, DPA, DPO, AI, ICP, FN, HRB, ICO, Scorecard, Heat Map, Watchlist, MCP Server, Series A/B/C). Each gap meant a reader hitting an unfamiliar term and having to Google it. The model writing the dossier doesn't reliably catch this without an explicit scan command (the bash one-liner in `voice-and-style.md`). **Always run the glossary-completeness scan before ship.** Common gaps: §0 framework terms (BLUF, SWOT, Heat Map, Scorecard, Watchlist), business-register codes (FN, HRB, ICO, KvK, CVR), entity-specific product names, and modern AI terminology (agentic, MCP Server, AI agent).

## Stage / vertical / template lessons

22. **One-size-fits-all dossier loses signal.** A series-A devtools company gets the same 23-section template as a public-traded healthcare SaaS, even though the questions a reader actually wants answered are 80% different. The fix is `--stage=` and `--vertical=` modifiers that re-weight section emphasis and surface vertical-specific compliance/regulators/integrations (load `stage-templates.md` + `vertical-templates.md`). Auto-detect from /about + funding rows; surface inference for user override.

23. **Risk scan as opt-in misses the highest-ROI signal.** Layoffs / breaches / lawsuits / regulatory actions are the most actionable findings in a due-diligence dossier — but if buried in a generic §16, they get missed. The fix is an always-on §16.X Red-Flag Scan (8 patterns in parallel as part of Step 2; load `risk-scan.md`). Even a "no findings" result has value: tells the reader the scan ran and was clean.

24. **Confidence scoring without freshness decay misleads.** A "Confidence 87/100" stamp on a 2-year-old dossier is misleading; the funding round may have closed, the SOC 2 may have lapsed, the customer logos may have churned. The fix is per-source TTL with weighted decay (load `stale-detection.md`) — different source types have different freshness expectations (pricing 60d, funding 90d, trust certs 180d, etc.). Decay reduces composite confidence; recommendation surfaces soft / hard / no refresh.

## Comparison-mode lessons

25. **Year-over-year deltas catch what re-research alone misses.** Re-running /research-entity on the same entity 12 months later gives a fresh dossier — but the most useful output is the **delta**, not the absolute state. Customer logos disappearing, SOC 2 status changes, pricing tier removals, exec departures — these are the signals that warrant a portfolio review action. The fix is comparison-mode (load `comparison-mode.md`); `--year-over-year` auto-detects prior dossier and produces a 10-section delta report alongside the new dossier.

## Output / format lessons

26. **One artifact does not fit all readers.** The full 23-section dossier is right for the analyst writing the brief, but wrong for the C-suite reader (1-page exec summary), wrong for sales (battle card), wrong for the VC (memo template), wrong for downstream tooling (JSON). The fix is `--export=exec|battle-card|vc-memo|json` (load `output-formats.md`) — alternate condensed/repurposed views derived from the canonical MD. JSON especially valuable for skill-self-MCP-server (load `mcp-server.md`).

27. **Battle card without the user's own product context becomes generic.** A "battle card" by definition compares two products; if you only have intel on one, you can write competitive intel but not a true battle card. Always prompt the user for their own product positioning + 3 differentiators when generating `--export=battle-card`. If they decline, output as "Competitive Intel One-Pager" instead.

## Industry benchmark lessons

28. **Stale benchmarks mislead.** Citing a 2022 Bessemer Cloud Index in a 2026 dossier gives the wrong comp; cohort medians shift annually (and growth rates dropped meaningfully 2022→2024). Always cite the most recent benchmark report and disclose the as-of date (load `benchmarks.md`).

29. **Estimating NRR / Magic Number for private companies fabricates the comparison.** These metrics aren't externally observable for private SaaS; if disclosed by founder in an interview, label `founder-self-claim`; if not disclosed, write "Not disclosed → cannot benchmark" rather than estimating. Better honest gap than fake precision.

## Depersonalization & competitive-voice lessons

30. **"Outsider" labels are themselves a stylistic provenance leak.** A real production case: a dossier written from a position commercially close to its subject was rewritten with "Outsider verdict" / "Outsider observation" / "Outsider conclusion" / "(Outsider Sample-Inferred)" / "Outsider transparency penalty" — 18+ instances. A reader pushed back that the framing still signalled the author's relationship to the subject. The "Outsider" label is itself the leak — a truly external dossier wouldn't need to label its perspective; it would just BE one. The repeated labeling implies a contrasting "insider" view exists, which only makes sense if the author has the contrasting view. The fix is neutral labels (Verdict / Observation / Conclusion / Interpretation) and a renamed confidence dimension ("External verification penalty" not "Outsider transparency penalty"). Always run the 12-category leak scan in `voice-and-style.md` before shipping competitive/DD dossiers.

31. **Skill-self-references and slash-command refs are leaks too.** A real production case: the same dossier contained `/schedule cron="0 9 1 */3 *" "/research-entity ./..."` in the freshness section, and "The skill that produced this dossier explicitly audits for hallucinated claims" in the Glossary. Both reveal that the dossier was authored by an LLM running a specific Claude Code skill — provenance metadata that doesn't belong in a third-party external dossier. Use tool-agnostic language ("recommended cadence: quarterly review") instead of slash-command syntax. Never reference the skill, segment-file names, or slash commands in the dossier body.

32. **Editorial trail markers reveal multi-revision history.** A real production case: a confidence-score "what would raise the score" table contained "added to first-mentions in this revision" — a remnant from earlier edits that revealed the dossier was on its Nth iteration. An external dossier should read as if its current state is the only state. Strip "in this revision" / "previously stated" / "we added X" / "originally was Y" — these belong in git history, not the published dossier. The 12-category leak scan in `voice-and-style.md` catches this pattern.

33. **The External Verification Penalty (-5) is the load-bearing structural caveat for competitive/DD dossiers.** A perfectly-executed competitive dossier with all dimensions clean still has a structural ceiling because the author has no NDA-stage access — audited ARR, top-10 customer concentration, real burn rate, and cap-table internals are unverifiable from outside. Apply -5 to the composite (per `confidence-scoring.md` External Verification Penalty section) for `--type=competitive|due-diligence`. This caps composite in 70–82 band rather than 80–92. Do NOT name it "Outsider transparency penalty" — same stylistic leak as lesson #30; use "External verification penalty" instead.

## Professional methodology lessons (v2.2 — gold-standard intel-community + Big 4 + Klue alignment)

34. **Without ACH (Analysis of Competing Hypotheses), the dossier is single-narrative and confirmation-biased by default.** Heuer's CIA-developed ACH method (load `analytic-techniques.md`) forces enumeration of 3-5 competing hypotheses about the entity's strategic state, then matrix-evaluates each piece of evidence against EVERY hypothesis (aim to disprove, not confirm). For `--type=due-diligence|investment` dossiers, this is not optional — without ACH, the dossier reads as a sales pitch for one interpretation. The load-bearing diagnostic: when the matrix shows multiple hypotheses surviving disconfirmation equally, the dossier should explicitly say so rather than picking a "winner" by 1 vote.

35. **SATs (Devil's Advocate, Pre-mortem, Key Assumptions Check) are how serious analytic shops avoid groupthink.** Each adds ~30-50 lines but catches different failure modes: Devil's Advocate stress-tests the BLUF; Pre-mortem ("imagine this is wrong in 18 months — why?") surfaces non-obvious failure modes; Key Assumptions Check (5-7 explicit load-bearing assumptions with confidence ratings) makes implicit assumptions reviewable. ICD 203 mandates these for high-stakes US Intelligence Community products; Big 4 commercial DD adopts them for $100K+ engagements. Skipping all three for a routine dossier is fine; skipping all three for a board-ready DD report is malpractice.

36. **Admiralty Code (A1-F6) is the formal source-rating notation that intel-trained readers expect.** Our existing labels (`single-source`, `vendor-claimed`, `founder-self-claim`) are plain-English explanations of WHY a source is weak. The Admiralty Code (load `source-rating.md`) is a formal 2-character cross-comparable rating of HOW weak. Both serve different audiences: plain-English labels for general business readers, Admiralty Code for intel-community / NATO / Five Eyes / cyber threat intelligence audiences. They compose: a single source can carry both labels (e.g., Latka revenue = D3 AND `single-source / founder-self-reported`). Apply Admiralty Code automatically when `--type=due-diligence|investment` AND `--validation=max`.

37. **Klue's FIA (Fact, Impact, Act) framework is the dominant battle-card industry standard — enforce it.** A free-form battle card without FIA structure produces useless output: facts without impact (rep doesn't know when to use the intel), impact without act (rep can't translate to talk track), act without fact (rep loses credibility on first prospect challenge). Klue customers report 50%+ of failed battle cards stem from un-sourced "Fact" rows; Klue's Compete Agent (2026) AI feature enforces FIA structure to prevent this. Our `--export=battle-card` now hard-enforces FIA in every row (load `output-formats.md` updated battle-card section).

38. **Expert calls + customer reference calls are the primary-research layer that distinguishes Big 4 CDD from public-sources-only research.** A Big 4 commercial DD engagement averages 5-15 expert calls (Tegus / GLG / Third Bridge / AlphaSights at ~$1K-1.5K per call) + 15-25 customer reference calls (mix of vendor-supplied current customers, churned former customers, and lost-deal prospects). Without this layer, the dossier composite confidence caps in the public-source band (70-82). With this layer applied, the dossier moves into the NDA-stage-access band (80-92). `--export=expert-call-questions` and `--export=customer-reference-questions` (load `expert-calls.md`) generate the question batteries; the calls themselves are user-executed.

39. **PESTEL + Porter's 5 Forces remain the load-bearing strategic-analysis frameworks 40+ years after publication.** SWOT alone is insufficient for any vertical where regulatory/structural forces dominate (govtech, healthcare, fintech, edtech, legaltech). PESTEL (Political/Economic/Social/Technological/Environmental/Legal) catches the macro forces SWOT misses; Porter's 5 Forces (1980) catches the industry-structure forces SWOT misses. Auto-activate by `--vertical=` (load `frameworks.md`); McKinsey/BCG/Bain DD reports use both as standard, and our skill should match.

40. **ICD 203 expressed-uncertainty discipline (almost certainly / highly likely / likely / roughly even chance / unlikely / highly unlikely / almost no chance) replaces vague analyst phrases.** "Could go either way" / "significant risk" / "some indication" are forbidden in IC-grade analytic products because they're un-falsifiable. The standard 7-term scale maps each judgment to a probability range (almost certainly = 95-99%, almost no chance = 1-5%) so readers can compare assessments across analysts and time. Apply this discipline to every forward-looking claim in §0 BLUF, §16 Risks, §17 Strategic Analysis, §21 Final Assessment (per `analytic-techniques.md`).

## External-fact-correction lessons (v2.5 — primary-source verification discipline)

41. **Lead-investor identity is routinely misframed by lazier outlets — cross-validate against named-partner attribution.** A real production case: the dossier stated "Aurasell $30M seed led by Menlo Ventures with Next47 + Unusual" based on what appeared to be the consensus reading of the Aug 26, 2025 announcement. Multi-source re-verification revealed the **opposite**: Sacra ("led by Next47 with participation from Menlo Ventures and Unusual Ventures"), Tech Startups daily roundup ("led by Next47, with participation from Menlo Ventures and Unusual Ventures"), AIM Media (quoting Lak Ananth, **CEO of Next47**, describing himself as having led the round), and Daily Company News all agreed: **Next47 led**. Some outlets list all three as if jointly leading, which is where the error originates. **The diagnostic that settles ambiguity is the lead investor's own quoted attribution** — when a partner / CEO of one of the named firms is quoted as "we led the round," that is the load-bearing signal. **Rule:** require ≥3 sources for lead-investor identity, and prefer sources with named-partner attribution (CEO quote, term sheet press release) over outlet paraphrase. If the lead-investor quote is from a participating-not-leading firm, that is itself a flag to re-verify. Apply this as a hard pre-publication check in `--type=competitive|due-diligence|investment` dossiers where investor cap-table credibility is load-bearing.

42. **HQ location: the press release dateline is canonical.** A real production case: the dossier stated "HQ San Mateo" for Aurasell based on Daily Company News, but the official GlobeNewswire press release dateline read "SAN FRANCISCO, Aug. 26, 2025"; SaaS News, FinSMEs, BusinessWire, and the company's own site all said San Francisco. Daily Company News was the only source saying San Mateo and was clearly a transcription error. **Rule:** when secondary outlets disagree on HQ city, the **press release dateline is canonical** because press releases are issued by the company's own PR function. Cross-check against the company's own /about page and LinkedIn header; never rely on a single secondary outlet for HQ city in a competitor row. Bay-Area-specific gotcha: San Mateo / San Francisco / Mountain View / Palo Alto are routinely confused in transcribed business news (the airport vs. the city distinction in particular).

43. **Bundled-announcement funding rounds: find the primary source for the structural breakdown.** A real production case: the dossier consistently labeled Reevo's funding "$80M seed" (in mermaid quadrant chart, Tier C table, executive briefing) based on Reevo's own launch press release, which read "$80M in funding co-led by Khosla Ventures and Kleiner Perkins" — bundling seed + Series A together because both were announced simultaneously at GA. The **most authoritative primary source — Bloomberg — explicitly broke them down**: "Zhu Ventures … led the $10 million seed deal, and Khosla Ventures and Kleiner Perkins led the $70 million Series A investment." TechCrunch and TFN followed Bloomberg's structure; "Future Nexus"-style outlets followed the company's own bundled framing. **Rule:** when a company announces multiple rounds simultaneously (typical at GA / public launch), do not accept the company's bundled headline figure — search for the primary financial press source (Bloomberg / TechCrunch / Pitchbook / The Information) for the **structural breakdown**: which round, what stage, who led each, and on what date. This is especially load-bearing when the seed and Series A have **different lead investors**, which materially changes the cap-table interpretation. Format the result as `$Xm seed (Lead) + $Ym Series A (Co-leads)` not `$Zm seed`.

44. **Numeric figures attributed to a specific article must be verified IN that article, not just paraphrased.** A real production case: the dossier cited "5,800+ public MCP servers per Pento 'A Year of MCP'" in §0 BLUF and §10.5 ecosystem context. A post-publication audit revealed the specific 5,800+ figure could not be directly confirmed in the Pento article excerpts retrieved — the article discussed explosive ecosystem growth, but the precise number was not extractable from the cited source. The order of magnitude was corroborated independently (Astrix 5,200+, TheNextWeb 6,400+ on the official registry by Feb 2026, Taskade 10,000+ active by April 2026), but the specific Pento attribution was effectively unsupported. **Rule:** when citing a specific number to a specific article, verify the number appears verbatim or with the same magnitude in the article body, not just that the article discusses the topic. If the number cannot be confirmed in-source, either find a source that does cite the specific number or soften to a corroborated range with multi-source attribution. The leak pattern is "[topic discussed in article] + [plausible-sounding number] = false specificity attributed to a real source."

45. **Outlet aliasing for participating-vs-leading investors.** A real production case across both lessons #41 and #43: outlets routinely use ambiguous phrasing ("backed by," "with," "and") that conflates leading and participating investors. "Funding led by X with Y and Z" is unambiguous (X led, Y+Z participated); "Funding from X, Y, and Z" is ambiguous (could mean any of them led, or co-led). **Rule:** when the source phrasing is ambiguous, downgrade the certainty until a primary source resolves it. The sequence of preference: (a) press release explicitly saying "led by" → highest confidence, (b) primary financial press (Bloomberg/TC) breakdown → high confidence, (c) Sacra / PitchBook / Crunchbase summary → medium confidence, (d) secondary outlet paraphrase → low confidence, never publish lead-investor identity from this tier alone.

## Public-source verification + ARR triangulation lessons (v2.6 — primary-source hierarchy + Tetlock + ABSA)

46. **Vendor "X customers" claims should be cross-validated against marketplace install counts when applicable.** A real production case: a vendor self-reported "1,200 customers" via Latka. The same vendor had a Salesforce AppExchange listing with 89 customer reviews and a HubSpot Marketplace listing in the "100+ installs" bucket. The aggregate marketplace footprint (SF + HS + a few smaller marketplaces) implied 200-500 active installations × ~12% paid conversion (Atlassian benchmark) ≈ 25-60 paying customers from marketplace channels. The 1,200 claim survives only if non-marketplace channels (direct sales, channel partners) account for >95% of the customer base. **Rule:** when entity claims "X customers" AND has marketplace presence, cross-validate via marketplace install × industry conversion rate; flag the discrepancy in §16 if vendor claim is >50% above triangulated range. (`marketplace-signals.md` §3 + `arr-triangulation.md`)

47. **Customer-page logos require round-trip verification (logo wash).** A real production pattern: vendor's `/customers` page lists 30 enterprise logos. Searching `site:<customer-domain> "<vendor>"` reveals **0 mentions on most customer sites**. Direction-A evidence (vendor lists customer) without Direction-B evidence (customer mentions vendor) is structural logo-wash. Bain's *Diligent* commercial DD playbook explicitly flags this. **Rule:** every customer logo cited in §9 must have customer-side reciprocal evidence (case study, press release, exec endorsement, marketplace approval, or job-posting requiring vendor experience). Logos lacking it are flagged "logo-only — no public reciprocal evidence." (`source-hierarchy.md` §3)

48. **Source-tier discipline (T1-T4) prevents secondary-outlet propagation errors.** A real production case: the model accepted "Aurasell led by Menlo Ventures" because three secondary outlets paraphrased it that way. The lead-investor's CEO (Lak Ananth, Next47) was on the record saying "we led" — the **T2 named-byline source** that should have been the load-bearing evidence. The model failed to drive down to T1/T2 and accepted T4 paraphrase. **Rule:** every citation tagged T1-T4 (source-hierarchy.md §1); T4 alone is forbidden as sole source for any factual claim. (`source-hierarchy.md`)

49. **Time-sensitive claims need Wayback validation when sourced to current pages.** A real production scenario: the dossier cited "founded 2011" from the company's current `/about` page. Wayback's 2024 snapshot showed `/about` previously said "founded 2010"; the company tightened the date during a rebrand. **Rule:** for any claim sourced to a current page that is plausibly time-sensitive (founding year, customer logos, pricing tiers, founder bio), verify via Wayback what the page said when the claim was originally applicable. (`source-hierarchy.md` §2)

50. **Triangulation discipline beats single-method estimation.** A real production case: applying only ARR-per-FTE to a Series B claim of "$30M ARR" produced "$8.8M-$15.3M plausible" — a flag. Adding AE-quota × attainment ($9.6M) and marketplace-install × conversion ($7-12M) confirmed the same range. The triangulation made the flag defensible. Single-method estimates are vulnerable to method-specific assumptions; multi-method convergence is much stronger. **Rule:** for any vendor-claimed revenue figure, compute ≥2 independent triangulation methods before publishing the discrepancy flag. (`arr-triangulation.md` §7)

51. **Earned-vs-paid press distinction prevents PR-coverage inflation.** A real production case: a competitor dossier listed "12 press hits in last 12 months" without distinguishing tier. Investigation revealed: 1 TechCrunch (T1 earned), 2 Forbes contributor (T3 quasi-paid), 9 wire-distribution releases (T4 paid). The "12 press hits" headline overstated traction by ~10×. **Rule:** every press citation tagged earned/paid/forbes-contributor; report **earned coverage rate** (T1+T2 / total) in §15.X — that is the actual coverage signal. (`press-analysis.md` §1)

52. **Trust Center claims require auditor + period + scope verification.** A real production pattern: vendor's Trust Center says "SOC 2 compliant" with a Drata-hosted badge. The audit firm was unnamed in the public-facing copy. Investigation via the downloadable PDF revealed: audit firm was a small regional CPA, audit period was Type I (point-in-time), scope was limited to "production database servers" (excluding application code). Each of these reduces the rigor signal materially. **Rule:** every compliance claim verified for: auditor name (look up via AICPA Peer Review for SOC; IAF for ISO), audit type (Type II > Type I), scope (full product surface > carve-outs). (`press-analysis.md` §3)

53. **Negative-space scan catches what positive-evidence scans miss.** A real production pattern: a healthy-looking SaaS dossier passed all positive checks (good reviews, named customers, press coverage). The negative-space scan revealed: no security.txt, no public status page, no engineering blog, no SOC 2, no Trust Center, no audited financials. Each absence alone is a soft flag; six together is a hard flag for compliance/maturity gap. **Rule:** for `--type=due-diligence` always run the negative-space scan in §16.X using the standard absence-checklist + vertical-specific entries. (`analytic-techniques.md` §6)

54. **Tetlock-compatible probabilities make forward-looking claims falsifiable.** A real production case: dossier said "AI-native peer category creation is very likely to succeed by H2 2027" — the ICD 203 qualitative term was used but with no point probability. A subsequent quarterly refresh asked "was the dossier right?" and there was no way to score the prediction (very likely is 75-85% — was it 75% or 85%? what's the resolution criterion? what's the resolution date?). **Rule:** every forward-looking claim paired with point probability (Tetlock) + resolution date + resolution criterion. Track aggregate Brier across refreshes. (`analytic-techniques.md` §5)

55. **CAGE Distance Framework for international expansion claims.** A real production case: a vendor claimed "global presence" with offices in 6 countries. CAGE Distance from US-HQ to each market revealed wildly varying distance profiles — Canada/UK low, Brazil mid, Japan/Korea high. The dossier reframed "global presence" as "US + UK strong, Brazil mid-strength, APAC nominal" — much more accurate. **Rule:** when entity claims international expansion, apply CAGE Distance per market-pair before accepting "global" framing. (`regulatory-overlay.md` §1)

## Internal-consistency + framing-discipline lessons (v2.7 — post-validation findings on a real dossier)

56. **Pricing/numeric inconsistency between sections is a recurring real failure.** A real production case: a dossier's ARR-proxy estimation in §16 listed price tiers that did not match the canonical pricing section in §8. The downstream ACV math survived the inconsistency by coincidence (the seat-count assumptions absorbed the mismatch), but the price line itself was wrong against the dossier's own established facts. **Rule:** at Step 5, run `grep -nE '[€$][0-9]+'` across the dossier and reconcile drift. The canonical pricing section "owns" the prices; downstream uses must match. Same rule applies to headcount range, customer count, founding year, revenue figure. (`internal-consistency.md` §2)

57. **Version-label artifacts from prior revisions persist in the body and footer.** A real production case: a dossier shipped at v2.6 had a closing footer "End of dossier (v2.4 — full gold-standard methodology layer)" left over from an earlier revision. **Rule:** at Step 5, run `grep -nE 'v[0-9]+\.[0-9]+'` and reconcile any version reference that doesn't match the header. The header version is canonical; closing footer + any in-body version label must match. (`internal-consistency.md` §3)

58. **Audit-completion-rate framing routinely overstates what was empirically completed.** A real production case: the dossier sampled 10 customers for round-trip verification, fully verified 3, marked 5 as "identification-uncertain," and headlined "sample-verification rate ~50% directionally" — this synthesized 50% from bundling fully-verified + light-evidence into one rate. The 5 identification-uncertain rows weren't actually verified at all. **Rule:** when reporting a sample-audit "X% verified" headline, count ONLY items with full Direction-A + Direction-B evidence; report partial-evidence items separately. Honest framing: "3 verified / 2 light-evidence / 5 identification-uncertain" not "50% verification rate." (`internal-consistency.md` §4)

59. **Two-decimal precision on synthesized estimates creates false authority.** A real production case: the ABSA section reported sentiment scores like "+0.85", "+0.65", "-0.65" when the underlying analysis was qualitative inference from review-text patterns (not measured by NLP tooling on the actual review corpus). Two-decimal precision is appropriate for measured scores; for synthesized estimates, 1-decimal-plus-ordinal-label is the correct precision. **Rule:** match precision to underlying analysis depth. NLP-measured = 2 decimals. Synthesized text-pattern inference = 1 decimal + ordinal label. Inferred from indirect signals = ordinal label only (high/medium/low). Speculative = ordinal label, no numeric. (`internal-consistency.md` §5)

60. **LLMs are systematically generous when categorizing tiers / labels — default conservative.** A real production case: SalesTechStar (a press-release-republish trade outlet) was categorized as T2 "earned" when its actual editorial pattern is closer to T3-T4 wire-republish. The default-generous bias understates the rigor of the dossier when readers later discover the lenient labeling. **Rule:** when in doubt about tier assignment, default to the lower (more conservative) tier. Worst case: the dossier reader thinks something is undersold. The opposite worst case (oversold) misleads about the actual signal value. (`internal-consistency.md` §6)

61. **"Triangulation" requires independent measurements; methods sharing assumptions don't qualify.** A real production case: ARR-proxy estimation labeled three methods (headcount × $/FTE, AE-quota × attainment, customers × ACV) as "triangulation" when all three share the underlying assumption that the entity operates at SaaS-industry-typical metrics. They are three estimation approaches against the same premise — not three independent measurements. **Rule:** before calling N methods "triangulation," list each method's assumption stack and identify shared assumptions. If methods share >50% of their assumption stack, relabel as "multi-method estimation produces a plausibility-range band" — NOT "triangulated." (`internal-consistency.md` §7)

62. **Status-quo persistence forecasts default systematically too low.** A real production case: a forecast on "<entity> retains current branding 18 months post-rebrand" was set at 80% — generous-seeming but actually too low because most rebrands stick (~90-95%). LLMs under-estimate persistence/inertia for default-outcome forecasts. **Rule:** for any forecast about whether a status-quo state persists (rebrand sticks, exec stays, customer logo remains, pricing tier unchanged, etc.), start from base-rate priors (90-95% for rebrand-sticks at 18mo; 90-95% for exec-stays-12mo absent visible departure signals; 70-85% for customer-logo-remains-12mo) and adjust DOWN only when specific contrary evidence is present. (`internal-consistency.md` §8)

63. **Single composite confidence score conflates methodology rigor with factual confidence — split into two dimensions.** A real production case: the dossier's §23.1 reported a single composite "84/100 confidence" that mixed (a) methodology rigor (improvable via better techniques: Tetlock, ABSA, negative-space, ARR estimation, etc.) and (b) underlying factual confidence in the headline metrics (revenue, customer count, employee count, investor identity — all single-source). Methodology rigor improved from v2.4 to v2.6 (+2 points); factual confidence did NOT change because it's bounded by source quality. The single-composite "84/100" misled readers into thinking the headline metrics had become more trustworthy. **Rule:** for `--type=competitive|due-diligence|investment` with single-source / aggregator-derived / vendor-claimed headline metrics, §23.1 must report two scores: epistemic discipline X/100 + headline-fact confidence Y/100 — separately. The two dimensions move independently. (`internal-consistency.md` §9)

## Internal-consistency residue lessons (v2.9 — post-fix audit findings on the same dossier)

64. **Within-section count-vs-list mismatch ships even after cross-section reconciliation.** A real production case: a §9.9 customer-audit summary read "**3 verified** (Tenaska / EMCS / Crestcom), **2 light-evidence** (Choice Hotels / First Tactical / Headworks), **5 identification-uncertain** (Pinnacle / Mactech / M2 Logistics / Powertech)" — three counts paired with their parenthesized name lists. The bolded counts were 3/2/5 (sum 10); the actual name counts were 3/3/4 (sum 10). The number is what readers cite; the names are skimmed; the audit shipped twice in two consecutive revisions before being caught. **Rule:** when prose pairs `**N <label>**` with `(name1 / name2 / ...)`, count names in the parentheses and reconcile against N. The enumeration is canonical (concrete facts); the count is derived (must follow). Distinct from cross-section drift (technique #50) — this is within a single sentence. (`internal-consistency.md` §10)

65. **Terminology-rename residue survives find-and-replace when only the canonical form is swept.** A real production case: a v2.6 revision deprecated "triangulation" in favor of "multi-method estimation produces plausibility-range bands" (per technique #55) and renamed §16.11 accordingly. Find-and-replace caught the noun form but missed the past-participle inflection — §16.11 line 1964 still read "sits at the upper end of independently-triangulated ranges," contradicting the renamed framework in the very section it was renamed in. **Rule:** when deprecating a term mid-revision, sweep every inflection — verb forms (`-ed`, `-ing`, `-es`), noun forms (`-tion`, `-er`), adjective forms (`-able`, `-ical`), and compound forms (`independently-<term>ed`, `<term>-band`). Maintain a per-dossier deprecation log; re-run sweep at Step 5; zero unflagged occurrences allowed. Distinct from version-label residue (technique #51) which targets metadata. (`internal-consistency.md` §11)

66. **Adjacent paragraphs with overlapping content survive iterative edits.** A real production case: §23.1 contained two consecutive `**Note:**` blocks both starting "Mermaid diagram validation runs as a quality check during draft + export…" — one shorter, one longer (the longer one added a "presentation concern, not content-truth concern" rationale). The longer version was added in a later revision but the shorter was never deleted; both shipped. Pattern: revised disclosure appended without removing the prior version. **Rule:** at Step 5, scan for adjacent paragraphs sharing the same lead-in marker (`**Note:**`, `**Important:**`, etc.) within 3 lines; for each cluster, read both and either delete the redundant version or distinguish them with explicit lead-ins (`**Note (separate concern):**`). Bash detection: `grep -nE '^\*\*(Note|Important|Caveat):\*\*'` then flag adjacent line-numbers. (`internal-consistency.md` §12)

## Reader-validation + competitive-self-scan lessons (v2.10 — caught by reader-side validation that draft-time methodology missed)

67. **Parent-company leadership identity must come from the parent's own /leadership or /team page, not the acquisition press release signatory list.** A real production case: an Acme CRM `--depth=quick` brief credited HoldCo Group's founding to a single person — the executive quoted in the acquisition press release, with a second named only as the CTO who "spoke for the team". The actual count is **four co-founders**: the press release named three of them, and the fourth appears only in current HoldCo Group materials. **Rule:** when an entity is a portfolio company, source-of-record for the parent's founder/leadership identity is the parent's own /about, /leadership, or /team page — never just the deal-announcement-press-release signatory list (which names *the speaker for the deal*, not the founding team). Acquirer-side T1 sources include the parent's milestone announcements and the parent's /about page. (`competitor-verification.md` extension)

68. **Aggregator citations >12 months old in fast-moving categories require a freshness sweep before being cited as current.** A real production case: an Acme CRM brief cited an aggregate-ARR figure across 25+ portfolio companies from a 2024 founder-podcast interview as HoldCo Group's parent scale. The actual current-year figures were materially higher on every dimension — aggregate ARR, acquisition count, headcount and country coverage — a velocity uplift the dossier missed entirely. **Rule:** before citing any aggregator-derived (T3) figure that's >12 months old, search the entity's most recent milestone-announcement / press-release / blog page for refreshed numbers. For holding companies / serial acquirers / fast-moving SaaS categories, 12-month-old data is structurally stale. (`internal-consistency.md` Cat J technique #62)

69. **Headcount-narrative parent-network multiplier check — never run "small team → low velocity ceiling" as load-bearing analysis without acknowledging parent-network resources.** A real production case: an Acme CRM brief anchored its product-velocity analysis on "~25-person team can't accelerate" repeatedly across §0 SWOT, Strategic Playbook, §16 Risks, §17 Takeaways, and §21 Final Assessment. The same About page that gave the core-team figure ALSO disclosed access to a several-hundred-expert global network, spanning dozens of countries, via HoldCo Group. The dossier never acknowledged the multiplier. **Rule:** when entity is a portfolio company / subsidiary / division, the headcount-velocity analysis MUST disambiguate (a) entity-only headcount, (b) parent-network access, (c) parent-network practical applicability for the specific product-roadmap question. Quantitative form: `effective_velocity_pool = entity_team + applicable_parent_resources`. Qualitative form: lead with "Acme CRM has ~25 people core PLUS several-hundred-expert network access" — never just "25-person team caps velocity." (`internal-consistency.md` Cat J technique #61)

70. **For `--type=competitive` AND user is at a known competing entity, Step 2 source gathering MUST scan the target's `/comparison/<user-employer>-vs-<target>` URLs.** Vendors routinely publish a **dedicated `/comparison/<employer>-vs-<competitor>/` page** that names a rival directly, cites traffic or search-impression claims about that rival, and positions themselves as the cheaper alternative. When the requester's own employer is known, that page is typically the single most actionable artifact in the whole dossier — and generic Step 2 source gathering does not probe for it. **Rule:** when CLAUDE.md user-employer memory or `--type=competitive` is set AND the entity is a competitor, Step 2 MUST include a targeted probe of `<entity-domain>/comparison/<user-employer-slug>-vs-<entity-slug>/` (and the inverse slug ordering) AND a search of the entity's `/comparison/` directory for any user-employer-named page. The finding is a load-bearing competitive intel artifact regardless of any specific number it claims. (`SKILL.md` Step 2 — new mandatory substep #14)

71. **"Deal-close date not publicly disclosed" is often wrong; check legal-firm representation pages + secondary deal databases before claiming privacy.** A real production case: an Acme CRM brief flagged "exact deal-close date not publicly disclosed" for the HoldCo Group acquisition, treating only the company's announcement press release as the sole date source. The law firm that represented the acquirer and a secondary deal database both publicly date the close to a specific two-day window. **Rule:** before claiming a deal date, founder-departure date, or executive-transition date is "not publicly disclosed," search: (a) representing law-firm announcements (Goodwin, Cooley, Wilson Sonsini, Latham & Watkins, etc.), (b) secondary deal databases (PrivSource, S&P Capital IQ, Pitchbook deal records), (c) acquirer regulatory filings (10-Q footnote disclosures if public), (d) trade-press deal-roundup columns (PE Hub, Axios Pro Rata). Day-precision is publicly available far more often than the company's own press release suggests. (`internal-consistency.md` §17 technique #65)

## Additional anti-hallucination lessons (v2.11 — preventive techniques against slot-filling, claim-coverage gaps, and audit blindspots)

72. **Single-feature → category-claim extrapolation is slot-filling pressure.** A real production case: an Acme CRM brief observed "the AI email assistant is an OpenAI wrapper" (one feature) and concluded "Acme CRM has no AI strategy and 25 people can't catch up" (category-level claim). The brief never enumerated the entity's full AI / intelligence feature surface NOR checked parent / sibling-portfolio AI initiatives (two sibling portfolio brands had already shipped AI features). **Rule:** category-level capability claims ("X has no AI", "X has no security strategy", "X has no enterprise pricing") require: (a) enumeration of entity's full feature surface for that category — not just the one feature you saw, (b) check parent / sibling-portfolio context for the capability, (c) check trajectory signals (job postings in the area, recent additions, Wayback delta on the relevant landing page). Soften categorical claims to bounded ones with time horizons. (`internal-consistency.md` §18 technique #66)

73. **Absence-claims ("no SOC 2", "no proprietary data asset") need explicit negative-evidence — surface, snapshot date, search strings.** A real production case across multiple dossiers: phrases like "no SOC 2", "no marketplace presence", "no public Trust Center" ship without specifying WHERE the absence was observed, WHEN, and WHAT was searched. Negative claims are invisible to standard hallucination audits because there's no specific number to verify against. **Rule:** every "no X" / "absent" / "missing" / "without" / "lacks" claim must be paired with (a) the URL where the absence was observed (e.g., "no SOC 2 disclosed on [Trust Center](https://trust.example.com/)"), (b) the snapshot date, (c) the strings that were searched ("searched for `SOC 2`, `Type II`, `attestation`"). Acknowledge the asymmetry: never "X has never had Y"; only "X does not currently disclose Y publicly." (`internal-consistency.md` §19 technique #67)

74. **Quasi-deterministic verbs ("categorically beyond" / "cannot trivially" / "will never") are forecasts disguised as facts.** A real production case: an Acme CRM brief said agentic features were "categorically beyond Acme CRM's product velocity" — implying impossibility while ignoring that HoldCo Group's industrialized AI-pivot playbook across sibling portfolio brands makes 12-18-month delivery realistic. The verbs feel authoritative but make falsifiable predictions about uncertain futures without explicit probability or resolution criterion. **Rule:** the following phrases trigger Tetlock-discipline review and must be rewritten: "categorically beyond" → time horizon ("12-18 months out"); "cannot trivially / cannot easily" → effort estimate ("requires multi-quarter investment"); "will never" → Tetlock probability ("≤10% in next 24 months") + resolution criterion; "must / always / inevitable" → conditional ("if X holds, Y follows"); "impossible / no path" → bounded-difficulty framing; "guaranteed / certain to" → high-confidence Tetlock band (85-95%) + reasoning. Pre-publication grep: `\b(categorically|cannot trivially|will never|must (be|have|fail)|impossible|guaranteed|inevitable|never |always )\b`. (`internal-consistency.md` §20 technique #68)

75. **Competitor pricing cited inline must link to the competitor's own /pricing page (not aggregators or comparison sites).** A real production case: an Acme CRM brief cited "Pipedrive Essential ($24)–Power ($69)" and "HubSpot Sales Hub Starter ($20–$50)" inline without source citations. Competitor prices change frequently — these become stale fast and are also factually unverified by readers. **Rule:** every competitor `$XX` or `€XX` figure in §8 (Pricing) or §10 (Competition) must have inline link to (a) the competitor's own /pricing page, OR (b) a Wayback snapshot with the date of capture, OR (c) explicit "directional bands only — verify at <competitor>/pricing" disclaimer. Default conservative: state the band, then link, then disclaim. (`internal-consistency.md` §21 technique #69)

76. **Step 6 hallucination audit subagent shares the writer's blindspots; require fresh-fetch sample of cited URLs.** A real production case: the Step 6 audit subagent on the AcmeCRM brief flagged 3 issues but missed 5 material errors (HoldCo Group founder count, portfolio scale stale, headcount-narrative blindspot, AI-thesis blindspot, deal-close-date wrong) — all caught by the user's external validation. The audit subagent has access to the same context as the writer and inherits the same blindspots about source coverage, source freshness, and category-claim extrapolation. **Rule:** Step 6 audit subagent prompt must instruct: (a) re-fetch a sample of cited URLs (≥3 high-priority citations: parent leadership, headline metrics, vendor-claimed scale) rather than only checking citation existence; (b) probe specifically for absence patterns the writer might have missed (comparison-directory probe, parent-network disclosures on same source, T3 citations >12 months old, "not publicly disclosed" without verification ladder, single-feature → category-claim extrapolations); (c) flag categorical claims for Tetlock review; (d) acknowledge audit-quality limit ("I share the writer's source-context; reader-side validation against fresh sources may catch what I cannot"). (`internal-consistency.md` §22 technique #70)

77. **Claim repeated across 3+ sections is load-bearing — single-source repetition is amplification illusion, not corroboration.** A real production case: the "25-person team caps velocity" claim appeared in §0 SWOT / §0 Strategic Playbook / §16 Risks / §17 Takeaways / §21 Final Assessment — five sections, single underlying source (one about-page sentence). Repetition felt like reinforcement but added zero source corroboration. **Rule:** any claim used 3+ times across the dossier is structurally load-bearing. Require (a) ≥2 independent sources for the underlying fact, (b) ≥3 independent sources if the claim is in §0 Executive Briefing, (c) tag the load-bearing claim with all sources at first use; downstream uses can reference back ("per §X"). The audit must check source-count, not repetition-count. (`internal-consistency.md` §23 technique #71)

78. **Vendor-stated metrics with suspicious precision (96.4%) or round magnitude (40,000+) need "methodology not disclosed" label.** A real production case: the Acme CRM dossier inherited "40,000+ users" / "96.4% customer satisfaction" / "3,400+ monthly search impressions" as vendor-claimed but didn't explicitly note the underlying methodology was undisclosed. Both round magnitudes (40,000+) AND uncommon precision (96.4%) signal "marketing rounding" or "methodology not disclosed" — neither is independently verifiable. **Rule:** label vendor metrics by suspicion class: round numbers (10K / 50K / 100K+ / 1M+) → `vendor-claimed; rounded; methodology not disclosed`; suspicious precision (96.4% / 99.4% / 23,847) → `vendor-claimed; methodology not disclosed`; self-disclosed traffic / search-volume claims → `vendor-self-disclosed; not externally audited; methodology not disclosed`; customer-satisfaction percentages without N → `vendor-claimed; sample-size and methodology not disclosed`. (`internal-consistency.md` §24 technique #72)
